## Privacy at a glance

- **Built for learning, not for tracking.** Bodhya Learn works fully **offline**. Most information stays on the learner's own device.
- **You do not need an account to learn.** A learner can open either version and start playing. Without signing in, nothing about her is ever sent to us.
- **Signing in uses a parent's or guardian's WhatsApp number — never the learner's own.** To create an account we send a 6-digit code over WhatsApp to an adult's own mobile number, so that we know a responsible adult has agreed. **We never ask a child for her phone number.**
- **The app never reads your messages.** The adult reads the code in their own WhatsApp and types six digits into ours. Bodhya Learn requests no SMS or messaging permission and contains no code that can read a message.
- **You can erase everything from inside the app.** Open **☰ → My progress → Start fresh**. No reading, no internet and no email to us required.
- **No advertising.** There are **no ads** and **no advertising IDs** in this app.
- **No third-party analytics or trackers.** We do not embed any third-party advertising or analytics SDKs. WhatsApp (Meta) carries the sign-in message on our behalf and does nothing else — see Section 8.
- **No sensitive device permissions.** The app requests no location, camera, microphone, contacts, SMS, phone, or external-storage permissions.
- **We never sell or rent data.** Learner data is never sold, rented, or shared for anyone else's marketing.
- **Made for children — with consent.** Because our learners are children, a parent, legal guardian, or authorised facilitator enrols and supervises the child, and the WhatsApp code is how that adult's consent is given and recorded.
- **You can ask us to delete everything.** Contact us any time to see, correct, or erase a learner's data.

## 1. Who we are

Bodhya Learn ("Bodhya Learn", "the app", "we", "us", "our") is published by **Vishal Arya**, an individual based in Patna, Bihar, India. This is an independent, personally-run project; it is not published by any company or organisation. For the purposes of India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023, Vishal Arya is the **Data Fiduciary** responsible for the learner information described here. Under COPPA and the GDPR we act, respectively, as the **operator** and the **data controller**.

Bodhya Learn is a free, offline-first, bilingual (English and Hindi) learning game that teaches English literacy, Mathematics, Science, and life skills for Class 1–10. It is designed especially for girls in Champaran, Bihar, India — many of whom are early readers or non-readers who navigate the app by listening, because every screen can be read aloud. Bodhya Learn is used both in facilitator-run campus classrooms and by individual learners studying at home.

## 2. Scope of this policy

This policy covers the Bodhya Learn Android app (package `net.bodhya.learn`), the browser version at [learn.bodhya.net/play](https://learn.bodhya.net/play), and the backend service that I operate at [https://learn.bodhya.net](https://learn.bodhya.net).

<div class="doc-callout">

**What matters most is whether the learner has signed in.** Both versions behave the same way, and both can be used with no account at all.

- **Playing without signing in.** She can open either version and learn immediately — no name, no number, no account. Her work is saved on her own device and nowhere else. The app still contacts our server to ask for new lessons, which is an ordinary web request carrying no information about her (see Section 7).
- **Signing in.** An account lets a facilitator follow her progress and answer her questions, and lets her keep that progress if she changes device or moves between the shared laptops of a campus. Creating one requires a **parent's or guardian's own mobile number** and a one-time code sent to it over WhatsApp — see Section 4. Signing in is what turns on everything described in Sections 4 and 6.

The app is a self-contained web application (HTML, CSS and JavaScript) packaged for Android using Capacitor. It contains **no external assets, no content delivery networks, and no third-party code libraries loaded over the network**. Sounds are generated on the device, speech uses your device's built-in text-to-speech, and all learning content is bundled inside the app. The one-time sign-in code is sent to WhatsApp by **our own server** — the app on the device talks only to us, and contains no Meta or WhatsApp code of any kind.

## 3. A note about children

<div class="doc-callout">

**Bodhya Learn is intended for children and is used by children under the age of 13.** Because it is designed for a child audience, we build the app to comply with the requirements of **Google Play's Families policy** and to be safe for children. Because our learners are minors, the app should be set up and supervised by a parent, a legal guardian, or an authorised programme facilitator (for example, a teacher at a Bodhya Learn campus). By enrolling a child or letting a child use Bodhya Learn, that adult confirms they have the authority to consent on the child's behalf.

</div>

We practise **data minimisation**: the app is built to collect as little as possible from children, and only what is needed for the child to learn and for a facilitator to support them. We ask learners to choose a **first name or a nickname** rather than a full legal name, and we do not require an email address, photograph, precise location, or any government identifier from a child.

**We never ask a child for her own phone number.** The mobile number used to create an account is always a parent's, legal guardian's, or authorised facilitator's own number, given by that adult — see Section 4. This is deliberate, and it is not only a legal point: most girls in the programme do not own the handset they learn on.

## 4. What we collect

The information below is everything Bodhya Learn handles. Almost all of it is created by the learner as she plays, and all of that is stored on her own device. Whether any of it is additionally *sent to us* depends on whether she has signed in — the two right-hand columns answer that separately, and for a learner who never signs in the answer is nothing at all.

<div class="doc-tablewrap" tabindex="0">

| Information | What it is | Playing without signing in | Signed in |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **A parent's or guardian's mobile number** | An adult's own number, used to receive the one-time WhatsApp code that creates the account, to record that adult's consent, and to restore access if the sign-in password is forgotten. **Never the learner's own number.** | **Never asked for** | Sent to us and stored on our backend |
| **Consent record** | Which learner, when, by which route, and the exact bilingual wording the adult agreed to — kept as the record of the permission the account rests on. | **Never created** | Stored on our backend |
| **Chosen name** | A first name or nickname the learner picks to sign in and be greeted by. | On the device only | On device, and sent to us |

| **Learning progress** | Lessons completed and in-game rewards: stars, coins, and gems. | On the device only — **never sent to us** | On device, and sent to us |

| **Attendance / time spent** | How much time the learner spends in the app, used to record classroom attendance and study time. | **Not recorded at all** | On device, and sent to us |
| **Doubts (questions)** | Questions a learner chooses to send to their facilitator for help. | **Not available** — there is no facilitator to send to | Sent to us when she chooses to ask |

**How the WhatsApp code works.** When an account is created, we ask a parent, legal guardian, or authorised facilitator for **their own** mobile number, and send a 6-digit code to it over WhatsApp. Entering that code tells us that a responsible adult has the handset in their hand and has agreed — which is what makes the consent verifiable rather than merely asserted by the child. The same route restores access if the sign-in password is lost. The code is short-lived: it expires in **10 minutes**, dies after five wrong guesses, and is held only as a hash — never in readable form, and never in any log.

**If a family has no WhatsApp.** WhatsApp needs a smartphone and mobile data, which the poorest households in the programme are the least likely to have. So consent may instead be given **in person** to a facilitator, who records against the learner that it was given and how. Those records are marked as facilitator-attested, so they are never mistaken for a code confirmed on a guardian's own handset — they are different evidence and we do not pretend otherwise. No learner is shut out of the programme for want of a smartphone.

**When the device is offline:** if she is signed in, completed activities and doubts wait in a queue on the device and are sent once a connection is available. Playing without signing in queues nothing and sends nothing. Bodhya Learn remains fully usable offline either way, and signing in only adds the ability for a facilitator to see progress and answer doubts. Creating an account is the one step that does need a connection, because the code has to reach the adult's phone.

## 5. What we do *not* collect

<div class="doc-callout">

To keep this app safe for children, Bodhya Learn does **not** collect, request, or transmit any of the following:

- No advertising identifiers (no Android Advertising ID / AAID), and no SIM serial or device build serial.
- No precise or coarse **location** data, and no GPS access.
- No contacts, call logs, SMS, photos, camera, microphone recordings, or files from the device.
- **No phone number from the learner, and no email address from anyone.** The only number we ever handle is a parent's, guardian's, or facilitator's own, given to us by that adult (see Section 4).
- **No reading of the device's own phone number,** and no reading of messages of any kind.
- No government identifiers (such as Aadhaar), biometrics, or financial information.
- No behavioural advertising profiles, cross-app tracking, or fingerprinting of any kind.
- No third-party analytics, advertising, or social-media SDKs.

**App permissions:** the app requests **no sensitive Android runtime permissions** — it does not ask for location, camera, microphone, contacts, SMS, phone, or external-storage access. Speech is produced through your device's built-in text-to-speech, which does not require a permission.

**The sign-in code is typed in, not read.** The adult opens their own WhatsApp, reads the six digits, and types them into Bodhya Learn. We deliberately did **not** build automatic code detection: it would have required permission to read your messages, and extra third-party code inside a children's app, to save a few keystrokes. `READ_SMS` and `RECEIVE_SMS` are absent from the app, and the app cannot see any message on the device.

**About the AI tutor:** the app's code contains an experimental AI voice tutor ("Roshni"). It is **disabled and not active** in this release; it processes no data and sends nothing anywhere. If we ever enable it, we will update this policy first and, where the law requires it, seek fresh consent before it is turned on.

## 6. Why we use this information and our legal basis

- **To teach and adapt** *(signed in or not)* — progress, stars/coins/gems, and quiz/test attempts let the app show the right next lesson and let the learner resume where she left off. For a learner who has not signed in, this happens entirely on her own device.
- **To keep the app working and secure** *(signed in or not)* — basic technical operation and preventing misuse.
- **To obtain and evidence verifiable parental consent** *(signed in)* — the guardian's mobile number and the one-time WhatsApp code exist for this purpose, together with the consent record that results from it.
- **To let the learner sign in, save progress, and recover access** *(signed in)* — the chosen name, the campus login password, and the same code route if that password is lost.
- **To let a facilitator support the learner** *(signed in)* — attendance/time-spent and doubts help a teacher see who needs help, record classroom attendance, and answer questions.

**A learner who never signs in has no account and no data on our backend**, so the last three purposes simply do not arise for her.

Our primary lawful basis for processing a child's data is the **verifiable consent** of the child's parent, legal guardian, or authorised facilitator (India's DPDP Act 2023, Section 9; GDPR Article 6(1)(a) together with Article 8; and COPPA verifiable parental / school-authorised consent). The one-time code delivered to the adult's own handset is how we **verify** that consent, and the consent record is how we **evidence** it. Where relevant we also process data to perform the educational service the child was enrolled in. We do **not** use learner data for advertising, behavioural profiling, or any commercial purpose.

We also use the guardian's number to keep the sign-in route from being abused — for example the limits on how many codes one number can be sent, which stop anyone from flooding a family's phone or spending the messages it needs to get back in. That is part of providing the service the adult consented to, and for GDPR purposes it rests on our legitimate interest in the security of the service.

## 7. Where data is stored: on the device vs. our backend

### On the device

By default, and whenever the app is used offline, the information in Section 4 that the learner creates is stored locally on her device (in the app's local storage). If she has not signed in, this is the *only* place it ever exists. It can be erased at any time from inside the app: **☰ → My progress → Start fresh**.

The guardian's mobile number and the consent record are **not** stored on the device. They exist only on our backend.

### Google backup, on Android

The Android app's saved progress is included in **Android's standard Auto Backup**, so a copy is kept in the **device owner's own Google account** and is restored if the app is installed again on that phone or a replacement. This matters most for a learner who plays without an account: she has no copy on our server, so without this a phone reset would cost her everything she had learned and she would have to begin the whole curriculum again.

What that copy contains is only what the app has saved on the device. For a learner who has not signed in, that is **anonymous stars, gems, which lessons and tests were completed, and the app's settings** — no name, no email address, no phone number and no identifier of any kind. For a signed-in learner it also includes her chosen name and the hashed sign-in password held on that device. It never contains a guardian's mobile number, which is stored only on our backend. The copy is held by Google for the phone's owner under [Google's own privacy policy](https://policies.google.com/privacy) — **we cannot see it, read it, or delete it**, and it is not a transfer of data to us or to anyone else.

If you would rather it were not kept: use **Start fresh** in the app *before* uninstalling, so the emptied state replaces the stored copy at the next backup; delete the backup in the Google Drive app under *Backups*; or switch the phone's backup off in *Settings → Google → Backup*. Full instructions are on the [data-deletion page](https://bodhya.net/data-deletion).

### On our backend

**When a learner is signed in,** either version synchronises her data to a backend service that **I operate myself**, hosted at [https://learn.bodhya.net](https://learn.bodhya.net) using the open-source Frappe framework. This lets facilitators view progress and answer doubts, and lets a learner keep her progress across devices and across the shared laptops of a campus. The guardian's mobile number and the consent record live here too, and nowhere else. This backend is controlled by me; the data on it is **not** handed to advertisers, data brokers, or other third parties (see Section 8).

**A learner who has not signed in synchronises nothing.** The app does still contact the same server, but only to ask for lessons — it checks for updated course content each time it starts. Those are ordinary web requests that carry no information about the learner. Like any web request they do reveal the device's **IP address** to the server, which appears in routine server logs; we do not use those logs to build a profile, and we do not link them to any learner. A lesson video, if a course carries one, is streamed the same way. The app works completely without this: with no internet it simply uses the lessons already bundled inside it.

## 8. Sharing, selling, and advertising

**We do not sell, rent, or trade learner data. We do not share it for anyone's advertising or marketing. The app shows no advertisements.**

When a learner is signed in, her information is visible only to: (a) the learner; (b) the learner's authorised facilitator(s) and the volunteers who support the learner; and (c) the learner's parent or legal guardian on request. A learner who has not signed in sends us nothing at all, so for her this section is simply "no-one but the learner". We may disclose information only if we are legally required to do so by a valid legal process, or where necessary to protect the safety of a child. We do not transfer learner data to third parties for their own purposes, and we do not use any third-party processor for advertising or analytics.

## 9. Children's data and the law

We handle children's data in line with the following frameworks:

### India — Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 (and the DPDP Rules, 2025)

Under India's DPDP Act, 2023 a "child" is anyone **under 18 years of age**. Processing a child's personal data requires **verifiable consent from a parent or lawful guardian**, and the law prohibits tracking, behavioural monitoring, and targeted advertising directed at children — none of which Bodhya Learn does. Because essentially every learner is a child by that definition, we do not rest consent on a tick-box asserted by the child. It is instead either **confirmed on the adult's own handset**, by the one-time WhatsApp code described in Section 4, or **recorded by a facilitator** who took it in person — and every record says which of the two it was, so the evidence is never overstated. A learner who never signs in gives us no personal data at all, so no consent is required for that use and none is requested. Note: the Digital Personal Data Protection Rules, 2025 were notified on 14 November 2025 and provide an approximately 18-month phased implementation period, so key obligations become fully enforceable during 2026–2027. We will keep our practices and this policy aligned with the DPDP framework as its provisions take effect.

### United States — COPPA

For families in the United States, we comply with the Children's Online Privacy Protection Act (COPPA), which protects children under 13. We collect only the limited information described in Section 4, obtain verifiable parental consent (or, in a classroom, consent authorised by the school/facilitator acting on a parent's behalf for a purely educational purpose), do not condition a child's participation on disclosing more than is reasonably necessary, and give parents the right to review and delete their child's information. Our operator contact details required by COPPA are in Section 15.

### European Union / EEA / UK — GDPR and GDPR-K

For users in the EU, EEA, or UK, we rely on the consent of the holder of parental responsibility for children below the applicable age of digital consent (Article 8 GDPR; between 13 and 16 depending on the country). We honour the GDPR rights described in Section 10, and we do not carry out profiling or automated decision-making that produces legal or similarly significant effects on a child.

## 10. Rights of parents, guardians, and facilitators

Because our learners are children, these rights are exercised by the child's parent, legal guardian, or authorised facilitator (and, where appropriate, by the learner). You may:

- **Access** — ask us for a copy of the information we hold about a learner.
- **Correct** — ask us to fix information that is wrong or incomplete, including the mobile number on file.
- **Delete / erase** — ask us to delete a learner's account and data from our backend, including the guardian's mobile number and the consent record, and stop any further collection. Data on the device itself can be erased at any time from inside the app (**☰ → My progress → Start fresh**) or by clearing the app's storage — note that on Android, uninstalling alone leaves the Google backup copy described in Section 7. A learner who never signed in has nothing on our backend to delete.
- **Withdraw consent** — withdraw consent for future processing at any time; this does not affect processing already carried out.
- **Portability** — where applicable, receive the learner's data in a structured, machine-readable format.
- **Object / restrict** — object to or ask us to restrict certain processing, where the law provides for it.
- **Refuse further collection** — parents may review the learner's information and refuse to permit its further collection or use.

To exercise any of these rights, contact us using the details in Section 15. We will verify that the request comes from an authorised adult before acting, and we will respond within the timeframes required by applicable law (and in any case without undue delay).

## 11. Data retention and deletion

We keep a learner's data on our backend only for as long as it is needed to provide the learning service — typically while the learner is actively enrolled in the programme — and to meet any legal obligation. When data is no longer needed, or when consent is withdrawn or deletion is requested, we delete it from our backend within a reasonable period. We do not retain children's personal data longer than is reasonably necessary to fulfil the educational purpose for which it was collected.

**The guardian's mobile number and the consent record** are kept for as long as the learner's account exists, because together they are the record of the permission the account rests on — and they are **erased with the account** when it is deleted. **Spent and expired sign-in codes** are pruned automatically **30 days** after they are used or expire, and are only ever held as hashes in the meantime.

**How to delete a learner's data:** Full instructions are on our dedicated page, [bodhya.net/data-deletion](https://bodhya.net/data-deletion), which is publicly reachable and needs no app, account or login. In short: email us at [vishal@bodhya.net](mailto:vishal@bodhya.net) with the learner's chosen name and her campus/class, or the mobile number the account was created with (see Section 15). We acknowledge within 7 days and complete the deletion within 30 days of confirming the account.

**Data on the device** is cleared separately, and immediately, without contacting us: in the Android app use **☰ → My progress → Start fresh**, or clear the app's storage; in the browser version clear the stored site data for learn.bodhya.net in that browser. On Android, uninstalling by itself does *not* remove the Google backup copy — see Section 7 and the [data-deletion page](https://bodhya.net/data-deletion) for how to clear that too.

**A learner who has never signed in has no backend account at all, so there is nothing for us to delete.**

## 12. Security {#security}

We take practical steps to protect learner information:

- **The strongest protection is not collecting it.** A learner who plays without signing in holds no password, sends us nothing, and therefore has nothing that could be intercepted, breached on our server, or handed over.
- **Passwords are never stored as plain text.** Campus login passwords are protected on the device using **PBKDF2** (a slow, salted password-hashing function). We store only the hash, never the password itself.
- **Sign-in codes are never stored in readable form.** A one-time WhatsApp code is held only as a **PBKDF2 hash**, is **never written to any log**, expires after **10 minutes**, and is destroyed after five wrong guesses. Spent codes are pruned after 30 days (Section 11).
- **The sign-in route is rate-limited.** There are ceilings on how many codes one number can be sent per day and how quickly, and separate ceilings per source address, so that nobody can flood a family's phone or spend the messages it needs to get back in. A facilitator can clear a family's limit on the spot if it is exhausted.
- **Encryption in transit.** Every request either version makes to our server — synchronisation, sign-in, and lesson-content requests — happens over **HTTPS**, and so does the request our server makes to WhatsApp to send a code.
- **Data minimisation.** The app is designed to collect as little as possible, which reduces risk (see Sections 3 and 5).
- **Self-hosted backend.** Synced data lives on infrastructure I operate myself, not on third-party advertising or analytics platforms.

No method of storage or transmission is ever completely secure, but we work to protect learner data using measures appropriate to its sensitivity, and we will notify affected users and the relevant authorities (including the Data Protection Board of India, where applicable) of any personal-data breach as required by law.

Meta acts as our service provider for that delivery, not for its own purposes (Section 8). A family who would rather no number left India can give consent to a facilitator in person instead, a route that uses no messaging service at all (Section 4).
